# Radar-Based Fall Risk Assessment

## 1. Clinical Problem
- Falls are linked to gait, balance, mobility, strength, and environment
- Current tests are usually short, clinic-based, and episodic
- Real-life risk changes across time, fatigue, medication, sleep, and home context

---

## 2. Core Idea
Radar is not used to “predict one fall event.”

Radar is used to continuously measure functional decline related to fall risk.

**Goal:**  
Long-term, contactless, privacy-friendly mobility monitoring in a realistic home setting.

---

## 3. Clinical Logic
Fall risk increases when motor control becomes less stable.

This may appear as:

- Slower walking speed
- Shorter or inconsistent steps
- Higher step-to-step variability
- Left-right asymmetry
- Longer turning time
- Unstable sit-to-stand
- More trunk sway

---

## 4. Why Radar?
Compared with camera:

- No face or image capture
- Works in low light / night
- Contactless
- Suitable for long-term home monitoring
- Can measure motion, velocity, micro-Doppler, and body trajectory

Radar = privacy-friendly functional sensing.

---

## 5. Validation Logic
**Camera / depth camera**  
→ reference for pose, trajectory, gait, turning

**Radar**  
→ estimates the same mobility features

**Clinical tests**  
→ confirm whether features are medically meaningful

---

## 6. Study Flow
1. Invite older adults into realistic home environment
2. Ask them to perform daily activities:
   - walking
   - turning
   - sit-to-stand
   - chair transfer
   - nighttime walking simulation
3. Record with camera + radar
4. Compare radar-derived features with camera-derived features
5. Relate features to clinical fall-risk labels

---

## 7. Labels Without Future Fall Data
Early-stage labels should be clinical surrogate labels:

- TUG time
- Gait speed
- SPPB score
- 4-stage balance test
- Chair stand performance
- Fear of falling
- Prior fall history
- Clinician/PT risk rating

---

## 8. Modeling Goal
Phase 1:  
Radar → gait / mobility features

Phase 2:  
Radar → clinical functional scores

Phase 3:  
Radar → low / moderate / high fall-risk profile

Phase 4:  
Longitudinal follow-up → future fall outcome validation

---

## 9. Key Message for Doctors
Camera tells us whether radar measures motion correctly.

Clinical tests tell us whether the motion features matter.

Long-term follow-up tells us whether they predict future falls.
