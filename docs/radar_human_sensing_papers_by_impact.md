# Radar/RF Human Sensing Papers Sorted by Impact

Last updated: 2026-07-09.

This file is broader than `radar_human_activity_sensing_papers.md`: it includes radar/RF human sensing papers even when they do not release a public dataset. I ranked the papers by a pragmatic impact policy: foundational status and field adoption first, then venue visibility, dataset/benchmark reuse, technical milestone value, and recency. Exact citation counts were not consistently available from stable sources during this pass, so each item explains the impact basis instead of pretending every row has a directly comparable citation count.

Accuracy numbers are the authors' claimed headline metrics when I could verify them from a paper/project page or a survey table. `NR` means no reliable headline metric was found in the checked source. Do not compare classification accuracy, AP, localization error, MPJPE, vertex error, and captioning metrics directly; they answer different questions.

## Impact-Sorted Narrative List

1. **See Through Walls with WiFi! / Wi-Vi** (2013, SIGCOMM CCR; Fadel Adib and Dina Katabi) is ranked first because it is a foundational through-wall RF sensing paper and is noted in public profiles as receiving SIGMOBILE Test-of-Time recognition. It uses low-power WiFi-like RF reflections for through-wall motion detection and tracking. The checked sources did not provide a reliable headline accuracy number, so interpret it as an early feasibility and systems-impact paper rather than a benchmark classifier. Source: [publication listing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fadel_Adib).

2. **3D Tracking via Body Radio Reflections / WiTrack** (2014, NSDI; Fadel Adib, Zachary Kabelac, Dina Katabi, and Robert C. Miller) is an early MIT/Katabi milestone for device-free 3D body tracking with RF reflections. It supports 3D body or center tracking, fall detection, and pointing with an FMCW-style RF body-reflection system. A public summary reports 96.9% fall-detection accuracy; this is a higher-better fall-classification metric and should not be compared to pose/keypoint errors. Source: [WiTrack summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WiTrack).

3. **RF-Capture: Capturing the Human Figure Through a Wall** (2015, ACM TOG / SIGGRAPH Asia; Fadel Adib, Chen-Yu Hsu, Hongzi Mao, Dina Katabi, and Fredo Durand) is a major early through-wall RF human-figure capture system. It uses RF reflections through walls to recover a coarse human figure and support identity or motion sensing. The checked sources did not expose a reliable single headline accuracy, so interpret the result mainly as a qualitative reconstruction milestone rather than a standard classification benchmark. Sources: [publication listing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fadel_Adib), [ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/2816795.2818072).

4. **Soli: Ubiquitous Gesture Sensing with Millimeter Wave Radar** (2016, ACM TOG / SIGGRAPH Asia; Jaime Lien et al.) is included high in the list because Google Soli had both research and commercial impact for mmWave gesture sensing. It uses a 60 GHz Soli radar chip for fine-grained hand gesture recognition. A survey table reports 92.1% gesture accuracy; this is higher-better for the paper's gesture classes and split, not a pose or localization metric. Source: [mmWave human sensing survey](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

5. **Through-Wall Human Pose Estimation Using Radio Signals / RF-Pose** (2018, CVPR; Mingmin Zhao, Tianhong Li, Mohammad Abu Alsheikh, Yonglong Tian, Hang Zhao, Antonio Torralba, and Dina Katabi) is a high-impact Mingmin Zhao/Katabi milestone for RF-to-2D-pose estimation. It uses WiFi-frequency FMCW RF heatmaps at 30 heatmap pairs per second to estimate 2D multi-person pose through walls and occlusions. The paper reports AP 62.4 in visible scenes and AP 58.1 through walls, plus over 83% identity accuracy from RF skeleton clips; AP is a higher-better keypoint detection metric, while identity accuracy is a separate classification result. Sources: [project page](https://rfpose.csail.mit.edu/), [CVF paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/html/Zhao_Through-Wall_Human_Pose_CVPR_2018_paper.html).

6. **RF-Based 3D Skeletons / RF-Pose3D** (2018, SIGCOMM; Mingmin Zhao, Yonglong Tian, Hang Zhao, Mohammad Abu Alsheikh, Tianhong Li, Rumen Hristov, Zachary Kabelac, Dina Katabi, and Antonio Torralba) is ranked as the first high-profile RF-based 3D skeleton system. It uses a multi-antenna FMCW RF setup with horizontal and vertical antenna arrays to estimate 3D multi-person skeletons for more than 100 people across campus environments. The paper reports keypoint errors of 4.2 cm, 4.0 cm, and 4.9 cm along X/Y/Z, and also summarizes 6.5 cm horizontal and 4.0 cm vertical average keypoint error; lower distance error is better and this measures 3D joint localization rather than activity recognition. Sources: [project page](https://rfpose3d.csail.mit.edu/), [PDF](https://people.csail.mit.edu/mingmin/papers/rfpose3d-sigcomm-zhao.pdf).

7. **Making the Invisible Visible: Action Recognition Through Walls and Occlusions** (2019, ICCV; Tianhong Li, Lijie Fan, Mingmin Zhao, Yingcheng Liu, and Dina Katabi) is a Mingmin Zhao/Katabi RF action-recognition milestone. It takes RF signals, builds a 3D skeleton intermediate representation, and recognizes multi-person actions through walls and occlusions. The checked abstract claims comparable visible-scene accuracy to vision systems and continued operation when people are not visible, but it does not expose a single headline accuracy number; exact comparison requires the paper's detailed tables. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09300).

8. **Unsupervised Learning for Human Sensing Using Radio Signals** (2022, WACV; Tianhong Li, Lijie Fan, Yuan Yuan, and Dina Katabi) extends the MIT RF human-sensing line from supervised labels to representation learning. It studies self-supervised or unsupervised RF representations for downstream human-sensing tasks such as pose, action, captioning, and re-identification. The checked abstract claims improvements over prior RF sensing on multiple downstream tasks, but no single headline metric is exposed; compare only within each downstream task. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02370).

9. **In-Home Daily-Life Captioning Using Radio Signals / RF-Diary** (2020, arXiv; Lijie Fan, Tianhong Li, Yuan Yuan, and Dina Katabi) extends RF sensing to daily-life captioning. It combines RF signals with a home floor map to caption activities and object interactions through walls, occlusions, and darkness. The checked abstract did not expose a single captioning metric, and caption scores such as BLEU or CIDEr should not be compared with classification accuracy or AP. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10966).

10. **mmTrack** (approximately 2019; Chenshu Wu et al.) is an important 60 GHz localization and tracking system in the mmWave human-sensing survey. It uses a Qualcomm 60 GHz 802.11ad chipset and CIR/spatial-spectrum features for multi-person tracking and localization. The survey reports 97.8% identification accuracy and 16.24 cm median localization error; higher ID accuracy is better, while lower localization error is better. Source: [mmWave human sensing survey](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

11. **mm-Pose: Real-Time Human Skeletal Posture Estimation using mmWave Radars and CNNs** (2019, arXiv; Arindam Sengupta, Feng Jin, Renyuan Zhang, and Siyang Cao) is an early mmWave skeletal pose baseline used by later work. It uses TI AWR1642BOOST mmWave point clouds for 3D skeletal pose estimation on 2 subjects, 4 actions, and about 38k frames according to the survey. The survey reports 3.2 cm depth error, 7.5 cm elevation error, and 2.7 cm azimuth error; lower axis-wise pose error is better, and these errors are not comparable to activity classification accuracy. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.09592).

12. **mmPose-NLP: A Natural Language Processing Approach to Precise Skeletal Pose Estimation using mmWave Radars** (2021, arXiv; Arindam Sengupta and Siyang Cao) improves mmWave skeletal keypoint estimation using sequence modeling. It voxelizes mmWave radar point clouds and predicts up to 25 skeletal keypoints. The abstract reports mean absolute error below 3 cm in depth, horizontal, and vertical axes; lower MAE is better and should be compared only with similar keypoint definitions and datasets. Source/code note: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.10327).

13. **RFPose-OT: RF-Based 3D Human Pose Estimation via Optimal Transport Theory** (2023, arXiv; Cong Yu et al.) is a representative recent RF 3D-pose method. It uses an RF system and multi-view camera labels to estimate 3D human pose across basic indoor, occlusion indoor, and outdoor environments. The checked abstract claims higher precision than prior methods but does not expose a single metric; compare using the same pose-error metric when available. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13013).

14. **HuPR: A Benchmark for Human Pose Estimation Using Millimeter Wave Radar** (2022, arXiv; Shih-Po Lee et al.) is included for benchmark impact in mmWave pose estimation. It uses cross-calibrated mmWave radar sensors and an RGB camera, with 235 one-minute sequences and two TI IWR1843BOOST radars at 10 FPS. The checked abstract does not expose a single AP number; accuracy depends on the model evaluated on HuPR. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12564).

15. **ProbRadarM3F: mmWave Radar based Human Skeletal Pose Estimation with Probability Map Guided Multi-Format Feature Fusion** (2024, arXiv; Bing Zhu et al.) is a strong HuPR-era model with an explicit AP claim. It fuses mmWave radar heatmap features with probability-map positional features for 14-keypoint pose estimation on HuPR. The paper reports AP 69.9%; higher AP means better keypoint detection on HuPR and should be compared only with HuPR baselines. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05164).

16. **mmMesh** (approximately 2023, cited in the mmWave human-sensing survey) is included because you specifically asked for it and because the survey identifies it as a dynamic 3D human mesh reconstruction system from mmWave. It uses a TI AWR1843BOOST radar with 3.9 GHz bandwidth at 10 FPS and VICON labels, with a dataset of 20 subjects, 8 activities, and 480k frames. The survey reports 2.47 cm average vertex error, 1.27 cm mesh localization error, 2.18 cm joint localization error, and 3.8 degree rotation error; lower errors are better, and these mesh/joint metrics are not comparable to HAR accuracy. Source: [survey dataset/performance rows](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

17. **mmBody** (approximately 2022, dataset/benchmark) is an early large mmWave skeleton and mesh dataset preceding newer HMR benchmarks. It uses Arbe/Phoenix mmWave radar plus Azure Kinect/RGB-D for skeleton and mesh human modeling. The survey lists 20 subjects, 100 motions, and 200k frames, but no headline model accuracy in the checked source; evaluate it using the model-specific MPJPE or PVE reported by papers that use the dataset. Source: [survey dataset table](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

18. **M4Human: A Large-Scale Multimodal mmWave Radar Benchmark for Human Mesh Reconstruction** (2025/2026, arXiv; Junqiao Fan et al.) is ranked for its scale claim as a recent multimodal HMR benchmark. It uses high-resolution mmWave radar, RGB, depth, and MoCap mesh labels for human mesh reconstruction. The abstract reports 661k frames, 20 subjects, and 50 actions, but no single headline accuracy; compare methods by MPJPE, MPVPE, or PVE on the benchmark splits once released. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12378).

19. **DGHMesh: A Large-scale Dual-radar mmWave Dataset and Generalization-focused Benchmark for Human Mesh Reconstruction** (2026, arXiv; Rongxiao Guo and Qingchao Chen) is a recent dual-radar generalization benchmark. It uses FMCW radar, SFCW radar, RGB, and 3D HMR annotations across 360k synchronized frames, 15 subjects, and 8 actions. The abstract does not expose one headline metric; interpret it mainly as a benchmark for generalization under configuration shifts, and compare methods using its sub-benchmark metrics. Sources: [GitHub placeholder/repo](https://github.com/SPIresearch/DGHMesh), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22827).

20. **Argus: Multi-View Egocentric Human Mesh Reconstruction Based on Stripped-Down Wearable mmWave Add-on** (2024, arXiv; Di Duan et al.) is included as the first egocentric wearable multi-view mmWave mesh reconstruction claim found in this pass. It uses stripped-down wearable mmWave radars with 1 TX and 3 RX antennas for egocentric HMR. The abstract reports 6.5 cm average vertex error; lower vertex error is better mesh-surface accuracy and is not directly comparable to MPJPE. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00419).

21. **MM-Fi: Multi-Modal Non-Intrusive 4D Human Dataset** (2023, arXiv; Yang Bai et al.) is a large public multimodal benchmark. It uses TI IWR6843 60-64 GHz mmWave radar with RGB, depth, LiDAR, WiFi, and IMU for HAR and pose/action sensing. The dataset includes over 320k synchronized frames, 40 subjects, 27 activities, and 4 domains; no dataset-level accuracy should be inferred because downstream accuracy depends on the model and split. Sources: [dataset repo](https://github.com/ybhbingo/MMFi_dataset), [project page](https://ntu-aiot-lab.github.io/mm-fi), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10345).

22. **MMVR: Millimeter-wave Multi-View Radar Dataset and Benchmark for Indoor Perception** (2024, arXiv) is a large multi-view indoor radar perception benchmark. It uses multi-view high-resolution mmWave radar heatmaps for pose, detection, and instance segmentation. The dataset has 345k radar frames, 25 subjects, 6 rooms, and 395 one-minute segments; compare AP, IoU, and keypoint metrics only within MMVR. Sources: [Zenodo dataset](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.12611978), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10708).

23. **OPERAnet: A Multimodal Activity Recognition Dataset Acquired from RF and Vision Sensors** (2021, arXiv; Mahmudul Hasan et al.) is a public multimodal RF/vision activity dataset. It combines WiFi CSI, Passive WiFi Radar, UWB, and Kinect for daily activity recognition and localization. It reports about 8 hours of data, 6 main activities, and 2 rooms; downstream classifier accuracy should be compared only for the same modality and split. Sources: [Figshare dataset](https://figshare.com/s/c774748e127dcdecc667), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04239).

24. **RadHAR: Human Activity Recognition from Point Clouds Generated through a Millimeter-wave Radar** (2019, BodySys/ACM; A. Singh et al.) is an early public mmWave point-cloud HAR dataset and common baseline. It uses TI IWR1443BOOST point clouds at 30 FPS for 5 whole-body activity classes, with repository split durations totaling about 93 minutes. The survey reports 90.47% accuracy; this is higher-better for the 5-class split and should not be compared with pose or mesh errors. Sources: [dataset repo](https://github.com/nesl/RadHAR), [ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3349624.3356768), [survey](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

25. **mmDoppler: A Dataset for Multi-intensity Continuous Human Activity Recognition through Passive Sensing** (2024, arXiv; Arghasen Saha et al.) is a continuous HAR dataset and method. It uses a TI IWR1642BOOST radar at 77-81 GHz for 23,100 seconds of continuous macro and micro activities across 19 activities and 3 rooms. The checked summary did not expose a single headline accuracy; compare only temporal segmentation or classification metrics for continuous-HAR settings. Sources: [dataset repo](https://github.com/arghasen10/mmdoppler), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21125).

26. **mRI: Multi-modal 3D Human Pose Estimation Dataset using mmWave, RGB-D, and Inertial Sensors** (2022, arXiv; S. An et al.) is a multimodal rehabilitation pose dataset. It uses TI IWR1443BOOST mmWave radar plus RGB-D and IMU for 3D human pose estimation. The dataset includes 160k synchronized frames, 20 subjects, and 12 actions; pose accuracy depends on the model and split. Sources: [dataset repo](https://github.com/sizhean/mri), [project page](https://sizhean.github.io/mri), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08394).

27. **MARS: mmWave-based Assistive Rehabilitation System for Smart Healthcare** (2021, ACM; S. An et al.) is noted by the survey as the first mmWave rehabilitation dataset. It uses TI IWR1443BOOST and Kinect labels for rehabilitation movement assessment. The dataset has 4 subjects, 10 movements, and 40k frames in the survey table; evaluate rehab classification or pose metrics only within the same protocol. Sources: [dataset repo](https://github.com/sizhean/MARS), [ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3477003).

28. **CubeLearn: End-to-End Learning for Human Motion Recognition from Raw mmWave Radar Signals** (2021, arXiv; Y. Zhao et al.) is a raw mmWave learning method and dataset. It uses TI IWR6843 raw mmWave radar tensors for motion recognition from raw radar data. Comparison sources list 8 participants and 6 motion classes, but the checked source did not provide a reliable headline accuracy; classification accuracy is task- and split-specific. Sources: [code/data repo](https://github.com/zhaoymn/cubelearn), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03976).

29. **MiliPoint / MILLIPOINT** (2023, arXiv) is a high-resolution automotive or pedestrian mmWave point-cloud imaging milestone. It uses a TI MMWCAS-DSP-EVM cascaded mmWave radar for 3D point-cloud imaging and tracking. Comparison sources list 545k frames and 49 actions, and the survey reports 1.2% cumulative tracking error; lower cumulative tracking error is better and this is not a HAR accuracy score. Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13425), [survey](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

30. **m-Activity** (approximately 2021) is a real-time mmWave activity-recognition baseline in the survey. It uses TI IWR1443BOOST point clouds for activity recognition. The survey reports 93.25% offline accuracy and 91.52% real-time accuracy; higher is better, but offline and real-time settings should be interpreted separately. Source: [survey table](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

31. **PALMAR** (approximately 2021) is a multi-person activity tracking and recognition system under domain adaptation. It uses 79 GHz mmWave radar point clouds for multi-person activity recognition and tracking. The survey reports 91.88% activity accuracy, 6.9 cm single-person tracking error, and 11.5 cm multi-person tracking error; higher accuracy is better for recognition, while lower cm error is better for tracking. Source: [survey table](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

32. **mmSense** (approximately 2017) is an early customized 60 GHz multi-person detection and identification system. It uses RSS fingerprints from a customized 60 GHz platform for human presence detection, localization, and identification. The survey reports 98.75% presence classification and 93% identification accuracy; these higher-better percentages are not localization errors. Source: [survey table](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

33. **mID** (approximately 2020) is a COTS mmWave person tracking and identification system. It uses TI IWR1443BOOST point clouds for multi-person tracking and identification across 12 people. The survey reports 89% ID accuracy and 0.16 m median position error; higher ID accuracy is better, and lower median position error is better. Source: [survey table](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

34. **m3Track / Track** (approximately 2022) is a multi-user 3D posture tracking system with mmWave. It uses TI AWR1443BOOST Range-Doppler profiles for posture tracking in a 4-user scenario. The survey reports 42.4 mm overall joint tracking error; lower joint error is better, but it is not directly comparable to mesh vertex error unless the skeleton and protocol match. Source: [survey table](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

35. **mmGait** (approximately 2021) is a mmWave gait-recognition dataset and model. It uses TI IWR6843BOOST and TI IWR1443BOOST point clouds for gait and person identification with 95 volunteers and up to 5 people. The survey reports 90% single-person and 88% five-person accuracy; higher is better, and the multi-person setting is harder. Source: [survey table](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

36. **GaiCube** (approximately 2021) is a high-accuracy gait-recognition system in the survey. It uses TI IWR1443BOOST micro-Doppler spectrum data for gait recognition. The survey reports 98.3% accuracy; higher is better for the stated gait or ID split, but generalization across environments should be checked separately. Source: [survey table](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

37. **M-gesture** (approximately 2022) is a large public-scale mmWave gesture dataset noted by the survey. It uses TI IWR1443 raw signals, point clouds, and raw RDIs for gesture recognition. The survey lists 1,357 minutes of data, 144 people, 5 gestures, and 56k frames; classifier accuracy depends on the selected model and split. Source: [survey dataset table](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

38. **mHomeGes** (approximately 2021) is a high-accuracy smart-home arm gesture system. It uses TI IWR1443 Doppler/CPDP features for smart-home arm gesture recognition with 25 volunteers, 10 gestures, 22k frames, and 5 home scenes. The survey reports 97.96% accuracy; higher is better for that gesture set, and cross-home generalization should be checked. Source: [survey table](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

39. **Pantomime** (approximately 2021) is a gesture-recognition benchmark with many gesture classes. It uses TI IWR1443 point clouds for mid-air gesture recognition across 45 subjects, 21 gestures, and 23k frames. The survey reports 95% accuracy and 99% AUC; higher accuracy and AUC are better, but AUC measures ranking/separation rather than exact class hit rate. Source: [survey table](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

40. **mmASL** (approximately 2020) is a mmWave sign-language and HCI milestone. It uses NI plus SiBeam 60 GHz FPGA software radio Doppler spectra for American Sign Language recognition. The survey reports 87% accuracy; higher is better for the ASL classes, but compare only under the same signer and split protocol. Source: [survey table](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

41. **WiSee: Whole-Home Gesture Recognition Using Wireless Signals** (2013, MobiCom; Qifan Pu et al.) is an early WiFi gesture sensing system that works through walls. It uses WiFi Doppler shifts for whole-home gesture recognition in office and apartment settings with 5 users in a public summary. The public summary reports 94% average accuracy for 9 gestures, but under 60% with 4 interfering people; higher is better, and performance depends strongly on interference. Source: [Wired summary](https://www.wired.com/story/wisee).

42. **WiGest: A Ubiquitous WiFi-based Gesture Recognition System** (2015, arXiv; Heba Abdelnasser, Moustafa Youssef, and Khaled A. Harras) is a commodity WiFi gesture-recognition baseline. It uses standard WiFi RSS changes for in-air gesture recognition in office and apartment experiments. The abstract reports 87.5% primitive detection with one AP, 96% with three APs, and 96% multimedia classification accuracy; higher is better, and the number of APs changes the operating condition. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.04301).

43. **EI** (approximately 2020) is an environment-independent 60 GHz HAR system in the survey. It uses a COTS 60 GHz mmWave transceiver and CIR data for activity recognition across multiple users and environments. The survey reports about 65% accuracy; higher is better, but the lower number reflects a harder cross-environment setting than many lab-only results. Source: [survey table](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

44. **SPARCS** (approximately 2020) is an ISAC-style 802.11ay activity-sensing system. It uses mm-FLEX or IEEE 802.11ay CIR data for activity recognition while preserving communications. The survey reports 90% accuracy; higher is better for its activity set, and the key impact is that sensing shares communication packets. Source: [survey table](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

45. **DISC: A Dataset for Integrated Sensing and Communication in mmWave Systems** (2023, arXiv; A. Shrestha et al.) is a 60 GHz ISAC dataset relevant to activity and gait sensing. It uses a 60 GHz IEEE 802.11ay SDR testbed for activity sensing, gait analysis, and ISAC. The dataset has more than 2 hours, 7 subjects, 5 activities, and 2 environments; downstream accuracy depends on the selected sensing model. Sources: [dataset DOI](https://dx.doi.org/10.21227/2gm7-9z72), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09469).

46. **CI4R-MULTI3** (2020, SPIE; CI4R group) is a synchronized multi-radar public dataset. It uses XeThru 10 GHz UWB, Ancortek 24 GHz FMCW, and TI IWR1443BOOST 77 GHz FMCW radars for activity and gait recognition. The dataset has 11 activities or gaits, 6 participants, and about 60 samples per class per sensor; accuracy depends on the model and sensor combination. Sources: [dataset repo](https://github.com/ci4r/CI4R-MULTI3), [SPIE DOI](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.2559155).

47. **University of Glasgow radar signatures of human activities** is a public radar micro-Doppler activity dataset with multiple facilities. It uses an Ancortek 5.8 GHz FMCW radar for human activity and simulated fall signatures. The record includes 5 main activities plus optional simulated fall across multiple rooms or facilities; recognition accuracy must be taken from papers using the data. Sources: [dataset DOI](https://doi.org/10.5525/gla.researchdata.848), [dataset record](https://researchdata.gla.ac.uk/848/).

48. **DIAT-uRadHAR radar micro-Doppler dataset** is a public suspicious-activity micro-Doppler dataset. It uses CW radar micro-Doppler for suspicious activity recognition, but the accessible sources did not expose reliable scale or class count metadata. Treat it as a dataset source, not a single benchmark accuracy; later model metrics need separate extraction. Sources: [IEEE DataPort](https://ieee-dataport.org/documents/diat-%CE%BCradhar-radar-micro-doppler-signature-dataset-human-suspicious-activity-recognition), [AIRHAR/RadMamba repo](https://github.com/lab-emi/AIRHAR).

49. **SUPER: Seated Upper Body Pose Estimation using mmWave Radars** (2024, arXiv; Bo Zhang et al.) is a seated upper-body mesh and pose benchmark. It uses dual close-proximity mmWave radars for seated upper-body SMPL pose estimation across multiple subjects and motion sequences. The abstract reports 30-184% improvement over a baseline, but no absolute metric in the checked abstract; relative improvement should not be compared to absolute MPJPE or PVE until the full metric table is extracted. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02455).

50. **Towards Weather-Robust 3D Human Body Reconstruction: Millimeter-Wave Radar-Based Dataset, Benchmark, and Multi-Modal Fusion / ImmFusion** (2024, arXiv; Anjun Chen et al.) is a weather-robust mmWave-RGB body reconstruction benchmark. It combines mmWave radar point clouds with RGB(D) for 3D skeleton and mesh reconstruction under different weather conditions. The checked abstract did not expose a single headline metric; reconstruction quality should be read through MPJPE or PVE under the weather splits. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04851).

51. **mmFace** (approximately 2021) is a COTS mmWave face and biometric sensing system. It uses a TI IWR1642BOOST radar with phase/amplitude SAR-style face imaging for face authentication and anti-spoofing. The survey reports 96% average authentication success rate and lower than 5% equal error rate; higher ASR is better, lower EER is better, and these biometric metrics are not activity-recognition accuracy. Source: [survey table](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.03149).

## Accuracy Reading Guide

- Classification accuracy, ASR, ID accuracy, and gesture accuracy are higher-better percentages for the paper's own labels and split. They are usually not comparable across papers unless the dataset, split, subject protocol, and class list match.
- AP is a higher-better detection or keypoint metric. It is stricter than simple classification accuracy and depends on the keypoint or detection threshold.
- Localization error, MPJPE, MAE, vertex error, mesh localization error, and tracking error are lower-better distance errors, usually in cm or mm.
- Relative improvement, such as "30-184% better than baseline", should not be compared to absolute errors until the paper's baseline and absolute metric are extracted.
- Dataset-only papers can be high impact even with `NR` accuracy because their main contribution is a benchmark, sensor setup, scale, or public data release.
